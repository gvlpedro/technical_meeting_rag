Architecture sync — Order Fulfillment Platform, June 2026.

We're introducing a new component, Refund Service, this month. Refund Service is entirely new — it
does not exist in any form yet. Refund Service subscribes to Payment Confirmed; when a customer
requests a refund for a confirmed payment, Refund Service issues the refund against the original
charge.

As soon as a refund is issued, Refund Service publishes a new data contract, Refund Issued. Refund
Issued is a brand-new contract — it has never existed before today. Refund Service is the producer
of Refund Issued. Refund Issued carries three required fields: refund_id, a required string
uniquely identifying the refund; payment_id, a required string linking back to the original
payment; and amount_refunded, a required number giving the refunded amount in cents. No component
consumes Refund Issued yet today — that integration is intentionally deferred to a later phase, so
as of this launch it has no consumer wired up.

We built Refund Service as its own new component, instead of adding refund logic directly inside
Payment Service, because the team wants refund processing — with its own approval and audit
requirements — to evolve independently of the charge flow itself; the alternative of extending
Payment Service directly was considered and rejected for that reason. The consequence of this
decision is a new dependency: Refund Service can only issue a refund once it has confirmed the
original charge actually exists, which it always resolves through Payment Confirmed.

Standing conventions for this platform, restated every meeting so there's no ambiguity: this
decision is owned by the Order Fulfillment platform team, its decision-record status is
Accepted, and it does not supersede or get superseded by any other decision — no prior ADR
constrains it either. Alternatives were evaluated primarily on operational risk and long-term
maintainability, weighted equally above short-term delivery speed. The team will revisit this
decision if the migration causes data drift or reliability issues it can't reconcile with the
current approach. Implementation includes updating monitoring dashboards, on-call runbooks, and
internal documentation for every new or changed component and contract described above; each
migration stage is considered complete once its target components pass the existing test suite
with no new failures, and overall success is measured by zero data loss and no increase in
checkout error rate versus the pre-migration baseline. Every new data contract on this platform
is single-tenant to the Order Fulfillment platform, uses a kebab-case identifier matching its
event name, starts at semantic version 1.0.0 with lifecycle status active, is delivered over
Kafka, is retained for 90 days, targets 99.9% availability, and is owned by the Order
Fulfillment platform team.
